# Time
We start off with the clocks we first hear in Speak to Me, which are "waking up" the narrator. After the brief encounter with losing control in the short term in On The Run, Time just tells you the severity of how much "long-term control" you have lost.

The clocks and the drums note the passing of Time, that has always been happening.
The opening chord progression paired with the two minutes of drums, is meant to slow down the pace of thought, or "waste time", going from a fast anxious pace in On The Run to a calm and more nuanced pace now. It sets the stage for a bright sunny morning, as you wake up, and look around and enjoy a slow day, maybe a weekend (a dull day).
It hits you:

``` 
Ticking away, the moments that make up a dull day,
You fritter and waste the hours in an offhand way.
```
The narrator brings to his own attention and to the listeners, about how much time is spent not doing "anything", just passing time, just existing. The moments tick away, with the passing of time, unstopping and constant, and you "fritter" about and waste those moments, not achieving anything, not doing anything, thinking that you have a lot of days, a lot of time, when in reality you have no time practically, life is short.

```
Kicking around on a piece of ground in your hometown,
Waiting for someone or something to show you the way.
```
Ironically, wasting time by reiterating the same idea, you waste your time, doing random stuff, mainly in your youth, waiting for a better opportunity. You spend your youth preparing for adulthood, your adulthood preparing for a better circumstance, and end up doing nothing, just wasting time. As Waters said, "I had the strangest feeling growing up - and I know a lot of people share this - that childhood and adolescence and one’s early adult life are preparing for something that’s going to happen later."

The narrator calls on you to realise that you are living and spending time from your lifespan doing nothing. As Seneca wrote in 49AD, in "On the Shortness of Life", "It is not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste a lot of it."

As the music changes with a backing chorus to give a haunting effect, Wright continues
```
Tired of lying in the sushine, staying home to watch the rain.
```
This can be taken literally as in the narrator has given up on outside/worldly endeavours, to stay home and do nothing, which by virtue of this overthinking he is also continuing on into. But it could also be taken in the sense that "sunshine" or good times are often ignored and overshadowed by our worries ("watch") of the bad times ("the rain").
```
You are young and life is long, and there is time to kill today.
```
This is the setup to the next line, convincing yourself, that a lot of time in life is left, even if you waste a little today its not that deep, you can work tomorrow, you can achieve greatness later.
```
And then one day you find, ten years have got behind you.
```
The gut punch, where taking each day as it came, and thinking there is a lot of days led to wasting them all, and as now the narrator finds, 10 years has got behind him, he is exactly where he was 10 years ago, just 10 years older. Life has been happening since you were born, you just never realised it and took control of it, you became a passenger in your own life. As Heidegger argues, we can become absorbed in the anonymous routines of everyday existence, living according to what “one” does rather than choosing our own possibilities. Or going back to Seneca, "While we are postponing, life speeds by."
```
No one told you when to run, you missed the starting gun.
```
The narrator comes to the realisation that life has always been happening and he never realised it, he calls on you to not miss that starting gun, not to let life pass by and grip it. The narrator begs you to run, to live your life, to not waste it, or else, like him you'll end up 10 years later looking back on a wasted decade.

I have no clue if the guitar solo is supposed to have any meaning but we should take a quick two minute break, and go listen to it, just to appreciate it.
The sudden pace increase in the solo reminds you time is still passing, dont let it slip away, recognise it, embrace the passing of time, and maximise it.

Gilmour kicks in with:
```
And you run, and you run, to catch up with the sun but it's sinking
Racing around to come up behind you again.
```
The narrator realises the effect of his words on you, as you will now try to chase the lost time, but you can't, you cannot stop the sun, you cannot catch it, it will keep going, time spent chasing lost time is more lost time, it will keep coming up behind you again.

The next line in my opinion deserves a place in the Louvre:
```
The sun is the same, in a relative way, but you're older.
Shorter of breath and one day closer to death.
```
Chasing lost time has resulted in nothing, the sun is still the same, it'll keep going round and round, it is more or less the same. The only difference is you are older, the time is still passing, no matter how much you try to catch up, everyday you get one day older and one day closer to death.

This also refers back to 
```c
"Dig the hole, forget the sun"
```
Where the narrator is so caught up in his day to day that he has forgotten to note the passage of time, i.e. the sun coming and going around. But now he notices, he pays attention, he realises, he is moving towards the time.
A similar thought is expressed in "Kabhie Kisi Ko Muqammal Jahan", written by Nida Fazli around the same time, in the line 
```
बुज़ा सका है भला कौन वक्त के शोले
ये ऐसी आग है जिस मे धुआ नही मिलता
```
Where the poet says that time always passes no one can stop it, most dont even notice it, but it keeps passing.

```
Every year is getting shorter, never seem to find the time
```
As you get older, you feel time speeding up, years pass quicker, and you realise that you wasted the time you were going to remember the most, and experience the most.

```
Plans that either come to naught
Or half a page of scribbled lines
```
Too often, we let time pass by, and dont act upon our passions, and at the end of our lives, we just have a list of plans that we never executed, stuff we didnt do, time we wasted. The narrator is pleading you to wake up and take control. Chase your muse before it and you die.

```
Hanging on in quiet desperation, is the English way
The time is gone, the song is over, thought I'd something more to say
```
The narrator looks at himself, and sees he is barely clingling to life, not living truly, just existing in a state of quiet desperation. And he realises that he has to get onto work and taking control of his own life, as he says, the time is gone. And in it's essence he realises he has told you nothing, but one who wanted to listen learned everything from it.

<h3> Deeper connections to the album </h3>
The song serves as centerpiece 1 of the album the main thought, of not doing anything, of wasting time, which leads to the narrator trying to utilise his time later and we will get to that later.

## My short interpretation of the song as part of a thought process that the album is
As the person has now woken up, he looks outside, and to himself, and is overwhelmed by a sense of lost time. He realises his life has been nothing, he hasn't achieved his dreams, he is still stuck where he was a decade ago. He wants escape but he realises it's too late for him.
