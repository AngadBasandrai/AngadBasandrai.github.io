# On The Run

After Breathe reminds you that you are alive, as you get concious of your own existence, On The Run reminds you that you are not at peace, you are anxious and running, from what, to what, you don't know, but you know you are running.
As the thoughts from Breathe continue the narrator starts getting anxious about whether they'll ever be free of the cycle of working endlessly for others. Will they ever get to be themselves? What are they running from? Is it themselves? Are they so disappointed in themselves that they are afraid to truly face themselves? 

```
Have your baggage and your passports ready and follow the green line to customs and then to immigration
BA 215 to Rome, Cairo, and Lagos
```
This line in the background is a semblance of lost control, the narrator feels like they are not in control of their own life the same way at an airport you aren't in control of what you have to do.

```
Live for today, gone tomorrow, that's me
``` 
Presumably talking after on the plane, the narrator reminds us that life is short, to merely recognise your existence and the change you need to bring is not enough, you have to act on that as soon as you can.

The plane crashing sounds at the end says that the plane on which he was running away is gone, he is no longer running, the narrator is ready to face himself. This he will do in "Time".

<h3> Deeper connections to the album </h3>
This song serves as the short interlude between two profound chains of thoughts, in Breathe and Time, where there is realisation of anxiety, of nervousness, of lost control.

## My short interpretation of the song as part of a thought process that the album is
As the dream starts to come to a close, the person is drifting in a state of half-asleepness, where he knows he is asleep but cannot change it, where he in concious of his dreams, as he realises he has no control over himself, he is anxious, he is running, he doesn't want to wake up, but he also doesn't want to lose his control to sleep.
